# The pilot pack — for the driver

**What this is.** Everything a driver needs to run Haulmeter on a real load, written for
them and not for us. Hand it over whole. It is the third document of the pilot set:
[`j1-journey.md`](j1-journey.md) is what we are trying to learn,
[`j1-pilot-runbook.md`](j1-pilot-runbook.md) is the operator's preflight, and
[`../WATCH.md`](../WATCH.md) is the observation protocol that governs the watched session.

**Read this before you hand it over.**

- **Part 2 is deliberately task-level, not tap-level.** WATCH.md's ONE RULE is *give the
  instruction, then say nothing*, and journey lines 1–3 all turn on whether a driver
  reaches a screen **unprompted**. A tap-by-tap guide to the money flow would spend the
  observation before the day starts. Part 2 says *what to accomplish*; Appendix A says
  *which button*, and it is sealed until after their first load.
- **Part 1 is fully explicit on purpose.** Nobody is measuring whether a driver can guess
  how to install a PWA. Setup friction that eats the twenty minutes is friction we paid
  for nothing.
- **Every number in here was read off the code on 2026-08-19**, not remembered: 120 minutes
  and $75/hour as the silent-rate-con defaults, 15% as the disclosed fee, five tabs, no
  self-serve password reset. Re-check them if you hand this over much later.

# Haulmeter — pilot driver pack

You are one of the first people outside the company to run this on a real load. Thank you.
That is worth saying plainly: **nothing in this app has yet recovered real money for a real
driver.** You would be the first.

**The app costs you nothing. It always will.**

Read this once before your next load. It takes about ten minutes. Then keep it on your
phone — Part 3 is the part you will want again.

---

## Part 1 — Set up before you go (about 15 minutes, on wifi)

Do all of this at home or at a truck stop, **before** you are sitting at a dock. None of it
is the part we are watching, so there is no reason to make it hard on yourself.

### 1. Open it

Go to **haulmeter.online** in your phone's browser. Chrome on Android, Safari on iPhone.

There is no app store download. It is a website that behaves like an app.

### 2. Put it on your home screen — do not skip this

**iPhone:** tap **Share** in the bottom bar → **Add to Home Screen** → **Add**.
**Android:** the app will offer to install itself. Say yes. If it doesn't, use the browser
menu → **Install app**.

Two reasons this matters:

- It opens and works **without signal**. You will be in dead zones.
- On an iPhone it is the **only** way alerts can reach you. Apple allows no other way.

### 3. Make an account

Email and a password of at least 8 characters.

> **Write the password down somewhere you will still have next month.**
> There is no "forgot password" link. If you lose it, only we can reset it, and that means
> messaging us and waiting. This is a real limitation of an early product, not an oversight
> we are hiding from you.

Use an email you actually read. We do not send a confirmation email, so a typo just means a
dead address on the account.

### 4. Answer "How do you run?" honestly

The app asks whether you have **your own MC#**, are **leased to a carrier**, or are a
**company driver**. This is not a survey question — it changes where your money goes:

| You said | What happens when you file detention |
|---|---|
| **My own authority** | The broker is invoiced **in your name**. The money comes to you. |
| **Leased to a carrier** | Same — invoiced in your name. |
| **Company driver** | The app builds the evidence pack and hands it **to your carrier**. Your carrier collects. |

If you are a company driver, everything else in this pack still applies — but the money
lands with your employer, not with you. You should know that before you spend the effort.

You can change this later under **More → Operating authority**.

### 5. Set your language

**More → Language.** English, Español, Русский, ਪੰਜਾਬੀ, Soomaali, O'zbekcha.

Set it **now**, not halfway through a load. If you read more comfortably in a language
other than English, use it — we specifically want to know whether it holds up.

One thing stays in English on purpose: the money terms quoted off your rate confirmation.
Those are printed exactly as they appear on the paper, because a detention claim is argued
from the contract's own words, and a translated contract term is a weaker argument.

### 6. Turn on alerts

**More → Notifications → Turn on alerts**, then say yes when the phone asks.

Alerts reach you when the app is closed: detention started, a claim paid, a booking
canceled. If you skip this, the app can only tell you things while you are looking at it —
which is exactly when you don't need telling.

### 7. Allow location

Say yes to location. "While using the app" is enough.

The app watches for you arriving at a dock and starts the clock itself. **If location is
off, it cannot** — you will have to tap "I've arrived" yourself every time, and if you
forget, the clock never starts and there is no money to claim.

### 8. Load your documents

**Docs → Add document.** CDL, medical card, insurance, permits.

Put the expiry date on each one. You get warned 30, 14 and 3 days before it lapses.

This takes five minutes and it is the thing most likely to be useful to you in the first
week, whatever else happens.

## Part 2 — On a real load

**This is the part we are learning from, so we are not going to tell you where to tap.**

That is not us being cute. If we show you, we learn nothing about whether the app explains
itself, and the app is the thing that has to work when we are not standing next to you. If
you get properly stuck, Appendix A has the answers — but try first, and **tell us that you
had to look**. That is the single most valuable thing you can report.

### What we are asking you to do

**Take one real load. Run the whole thing through the app.**

1. **Before you roll:** get the load into the app. You have the rate confirmation on paper
   or on your phone — start there. Do not type things in if you can avoid it.
2. **Check what it read against the paper.** It reads the rate con for you. It will
   sometimes be wrong. Nothing is created until you confirm it, and everything is editable
   at that moment. Look especially at the **appointment time** — your detention clock is
   measured against it, so a wrong appointment is a wrong claim.
3. **At the dock:** make sure the app knows you have arrived.
4. **While you wait:** let it run. Look at it now and then. If you get held up past your
   free time, the app has things it can do — find them.
5. **When you pull out:** you will have paperwork ready to send. Deal with it.
6. **If you sat past free time: bill for it.** That is the whole point of the product, and
   it is the one thing we most need to see a real person do.

### The numbers, so nothing surprises you

Most rate cons say nothing about detention. When yours doesn't, the app assumes:

- **120 minutes** free time
- **$75 per hour** after that

**If your rate con says something different, correct it before you file.** You can. A claim
built on the wrong terms is worse than no claim, because you only get to send it once.

### Tell us when you get paid

This is the one thing we cannot see. The app has no view of your bank account.

**When a broker pays a claim — even if it is three weeks later — go into Money and mark it
paid.** Then message us. That moment is the first time this product will have completed
what it was built to do, and if you don't tell us, we won't know it happened.

## Part 3 — What is real, and what it costs

### Everything that says it sends, sends

There is no test mode. There is no sandbox. This is worth being very clear about:

> **When you send a claim or a packet, a real email leaves the system immediately**, from
> `claims@haulmeter.online`, to the address you typed.

So:

- **Do not put a broker's real email in to "see what it looks like."** It will go.
- Put a real broker in when you actually intend to bill that broker.
- If you want to see the flow without sending anything to a stranger, use **your own email
  address** as the recipient. That works and it is what we do.

Nothing sends by itself. Claims and paperwork wait on the screen until you tap send. The
app will never contact your broker behind your back.

### The fee — 15%, and not collected

You will see a line called **"Haulmeter fee"** on recovered money. It is **15%** of what you
recover, and the app shows it so that the number you see is the number you keep.

**Right now we cannot actually charge it.** There is no billing mechanism in this product.
The app will say so, in those words. During the pilot, and until we build a way to collect
it and tell you first, **you keep 100% of everything you recover.**

We are telling you the fee exists because you would rather find out now than in six months.

### Your data

- **Your GPS at docks is recorded.** That recording *is* the evidence — it is what makes a
  detention claim hold up. It is attached to your claims and to nothing else.
- **Your photos and documents** stay in your wallet and go where you send them.
- **Roadside practice records your voice** to score your answers. The recording is scored
  and then discarded; only the score is kept. You are asked to agree before it starts, and
  "Not now" is a real answer.
- **We do not sell any of it.**
- **Accounts are never deleted** — they are tied to money, claims and evidence, and
  deleting one would destroy the record behind a claim. We can switch an account off. If
  you want out, say so and we will, and we will tell you exactly what is still held.

### Offline

The app works with no signal. It says **"Offline — everything saves to your phone"** and it
means it: photos, documents, arrivals and claims queue up and go when you have bars again.

You do not need to do anything differently in a dead zone. If something says it is waiting
to send, it is waiting to send.

## Part 4 — When it goes wrong

It will. That is what a pilot is.

**What helps us:**

1. **Screenshot it.** Whatever is on the screen, however broken.
2. **Note the time**, roughly. And where you were.
3. **Say what you expected to happen.** "I tapped send and it said Sent but the broker
   never got it" tells us where to look. "It didn't work" does not.
4. **Then carry on.** Do not tap it fifteen times — if something sends on the twelfth tap,
   we cannot tell what fixed it.

**What helps us most, and drivers almost never report:**

> **The moment you hesitated.** The screen where you weren't sure which thing to tap. The
> button you pressed that turned out to be the wrong one. The point where you thought "I'll
> just ask them."

Those are worth more to us than bugs. Bugs we can find. We cannot find the four seconds you
spent looking at a screen wondering what it wanted. **Tell us where you stopped, not whether
you liked it.**

And if you think something is stupid, say that it is stupid. We would much rather hear it
from you now than learn it from a driver who quietly stopped opening the app.

### If we do a call

Twenty minutes, on your schedule, and you get paid for the time.

**We will give you one instruction and then go quiet.** That is deliberate and it is not a
test of you — it is a test of the app. If the silence feels awkward, it is working. Nobody
will help you, nobody is scoring you, and every place you get stuck is a place we go and fix.

## Appendix A — If you are properly stuck

**Try Part 2 without this first.** Then come back, and tell us you had to.

The app has five tabs along the bottom: **Load · Money · Docs · Roadside · More**.

| What you want | Where it is |
|---|---|
| Add a load from the rate con | **Load** tab → *Add a load* → *Rate con photo or file* |
| Fix the appointment time | **Load** tab → *Correct the appointment* |
| Tell it you've arrived | **Load** tab → *I've arrived* |
| See what you're owed right now | **Load** tab — the Meter, running on the stop |
| Warn the broker before free time runs out | **Load** tab → *Warn broker* (appears with 30 minutes left) |
| Bill the broker for detention | **Load** tab → *Generate claim* → *Send it to the broker* |
| Add the broker's email | **Load** tab → the broker's row → *Add email* |
| Send your paperwork after a dock | **Load** tab → *Check and send* on the packet |
| See everything you're owed | **Money** tab — *Not sent yet* · *Waiting on brokers* · *Paid to you* |
| Mark a claim paid | **Money** tab → the claim → *They paid me* |
| Check a settlement for missed pay | **Money** tab → *Check a settlement for missed pay* |
| Show a document to an officer | **Docs** tab → *Inspection mode* |
| Practice a roadside inspection | **Roadside** tab |
| Change language, alerts, password | **More** tab |

**Two things that are not there and are not missing:** there is no map tab, and there is no
"forgot password" link. Both are deliberate for now.

## Appendix B — What "done" looks like for us

So you know what you are actually helping with. Five things have never happened, and you
could be the first person to do any of them:

1. A rate confirmation photographed by someone who isn't us, with the broker's details
   landing in the app **without anyone typing them**.
2. Those money terms read and understood **in a language other than English**.
3. An arrival alert arriving **on a real phone**, at a real dock.
4. A paperwork packet a **real dispatcher** says is enough to act on.
5. **A real detention claim, sent to a real broker, and paid.**

Number 5 is the one the whole product exists for. It has never happened.
